A full queue blocks producers, not consumers

The queue bounds the number of stored items by making producers wait when it is full. A consumer taking an item creates space; it does not mean that processing of that item has finished. [source]

This is backpressure: the queue makes the producer wait rather than accept unlimited pending work.

One possible execution begins with a full queue. Follow the wait and release. This is one possible ordering, not every legal thread interleaving.

Details and evidence

Producer

Calls put(item) and may wait for space.

Bounded queue

Protects its item deque with a condition variable.

Consumer

Calls get(), then processes the returned item.

put waits while full

put rechecks capacity after waking. Another producer may have taken a newly available slot. A notification is not a reservation. [source]

get removes one item; waits while empty

Removing an item creates queue capacity.

Calls put with the queue full

The call enters the queue's condition-protected section.

Waits for capacity

The wait releases the condition lock while blocked.

Backpressure

A mechanism that makes upstream work wait or slow down when a downstream resource cannot accept more work. Here it is implemented by blocking put.

Illustrative bounded queue

Example source, captured 2026-09-26.

def put(self, item):
    with self.changed:
        while len(self.items) >= self.capacity:
            self.changed.wait()
